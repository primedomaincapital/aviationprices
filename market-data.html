Market data · Business jet prices, charter rates, and pre-owned market figures

The last unpriced market in the sky, with numbers attached

New business jets have factory order books and published delivery totals. Charter has hourly rates that every broker quotes a little differently. Pre-owned has asking prices that move with days on market. Put the three together and you get a market that is easier to read than its reputation suggests — provided you keep new, charter, and pre-owned in separate columns, because they answer different questions.

The two charter guides disagree by thousands of dollars an hour on the same categories — light jets at $4,250–$6,500 in one and $2,500–$5,500 in the other. That disagreement is itself data: published hourly rates are broker marketing ranges, not transaction prices, and they exclude taxes, fuel surcharges, and positioning. The delivery totals, by contrast, are counted airframes with billed values, and they tell a steadier story: as GAMA put it in its 2024 report release, "for the first time in a decade our companies again exceeded $30 billion in annual billings and for the second year in a row, we shipped more than 4,000 units" — 4,118 units in total (3,162 airplanes plus 956 helicopters) against $31.2 billion in billings. (Source: GAMA 2024 Aircraft Shipment and Billing Report.)

Methodology

Included: GAMA shipment and billing totals for 2024, a published business-jet market estimate, and two public charter-rate guides shown side by side. Excluded: manufacturer list prices by model (they vary by configuration and were not verified as a comparable series), AMSTAT/JetNet pre-owned inventory figures (no public summary verified this session), and fractional-share pricing (no verified series this session).

Disclaimer: Aggregated public estimates for information only, not investment or professional advice. Charter rates exclude taxes and fees unless stated and vary by route, date, and aircraft.
